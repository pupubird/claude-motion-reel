**What it is:** a fintech wallet app shown as a set of physical objects. A real phone lies on a photoreal desk; its UI lifts off into 3D, and the screens become thick, curled, frosted sheets floating in a soft teal and lilac void. The "user" is a **small iridescent glass/soap bubble** that rolls over the UI, magnifies it, and reveals or changes values as it passes.

---

## 2. Why it reads high-end

The UI is never a flat screenshot. Every card is a **physical slab**: thickness, bevelled rounded edges, a soft contact shadow, frosted subsurface, and a subtle rim highlight. It is lit by one big soft key and a cool fill, so the whole film feels like product photography rather than motion graphics. The interaction is done by a **character-object (the glass bubble)** rather than a cursor or tap ripple. That bubble refracts what is under it with real lens distortion and chromatic fringing, so each interaction is also a material showcase. The camera is always moving on long, slow, eased dolly or orbit moves with shallow depth of field, and the scene changes are motivated (a whip off the desk, a particle burst, a page curl). It is bookended by the real-world desk, which grounds the abstraction and says "this is a real app on a real phone".

## 4. Signature moves (特效)

### 4.1 UI lift-off from a real phone ("exploded UI")
- **When:** 0.3–1.3 s; reversed at 21.7–22.6 s.
- **Frame by frame:** at 0.3 the screen is flat. Over about 0.7 s, the tiles (header, three list rows, buttons) rise off the glass to different heights, each **staggered by about 2 frames**, ending 1–4 cm above the phone in scene scale. They keep their original x/y, so the layout stays readable. 3D icons pop from the screen to orbiting positions with a small overshoot (scale ≈1.1 → 1.0). Shadows from the tiles land on the screen, which is what sells the height.
- **How built:** C4D/Blender, with each UI element extruded about 4–8 % of its width, rounded-bevel edges, and a matte white plastic material. In our stack: the 3D iPhone is already a mesh, so render UI tiles as separate rounded-box SDFs or instanced quads with a fake bevel normal map. Offset in z with a staggered `easeOutBack(1.2)` and add a screen-space contact shadow (blurred rectangle, 0.25 opacity, offset by z).
- **Why cool:** it turns "a screen" into "an object made of parts". The client brief says users feel texture, and this makes the texture visible in the first second.

### 4.2 The glass bubble as the interaction agent
- **When:** throughout (2.0, 3.8–8.4, 8.0, 9.6–10.9, 12.2–12.6, 17.0, 19.0–21.0).
- **What happens:** a small, perfectly clear sphere with **thin-film iridescence** (pink/cyan/violet bands on the rim) rolls across the UI. Where it passes:
  - it **magnifies and inverts** what is behind it (true refraction: see the "3333" seen through the lens at 8.0 s, and the refracted eye at 12.4 s),
  - it carries **chromatic aberration inside the lens**: the refracted eye at 12.4 s is split magenta/cyan by about 6–8 px at 720p,
  - it **changes state**: values tick up under it (balance counter), masked digits resolve (card number), the eye toggles from closed to open, and the avatar it touches swells.
- **Motion:** it rolls rather than slides. It travels about 1 card width per 0.5 s with gentle ease-in/out and a slight bob. It never moves fast, so you can always read it.
- **How built:** a refractive sphere (IOR ≈1.33–1.5) with a thin-film shader. For us this is our existing raymarched soap bubble. The new part is **using it as a lens over UI**: render the UI to a texture, then in the bubble's shader sample that texture with a refracted UV offset (`uv + n.xy * k * (1-dot(n,v))`), sample R, G and B with k × 0.97 / 1.0 / 1.03 for dispersion, and add the thin-film rim. State changes key off the bubble's screen position (distance field to each UI element → trigger).
- **Why cool:** it is the single idea that makes this piece. The product's "intelligence" has a body, and every interaction doubles as a materials shot. **This is Bub.** It maps almost one to one.

### 4.3 Curled-paper UI stack
- **When:** 3.6–8.4 and 17.4–17.6.
- **What happens:** screens are thin sheets that **bend like paper**: the top is flat, the bottom curls back. Three or four sheets stack with about 1–2 % gaps, each in a different colour (frosted white→pink front, teal middle, saturated green back). At 17.4 the edge un-rolls toward camera in about 0.2 s (6 frames) with motion blur.
- **How built:** a bend deformer on a subdivided plane. In WebGL: a vertex shader that bends the plane around a cylinder past a y threshold (`if (y > y0) { angle = (y - y0) / R; pos.y = y0 + R * sin(angle); pos.z = R * (1 - cos(angle)); }`), with R animated for the un-curl.
- **Why cool:** UI suddenly has material physics. The coloured back sheets give depth and a brand-colour frame without any HUD.

### 4.4 Card flip that shows thickness
- **When:** 9.2–9.6.
- **What happens:** the card rotates about its vertical axis about 180° in about 0.4 s (12 frames). At the edge-on frame (9.4) you see a **solid green slab edge** with a white specular line. It lands with about 3° overshoot and settles in about 6 frames.
- **Why cool:** the edge-on frame is a one-frame proof that it is a real object.

### 4.5 Spoiler-dust dissolve into a 3D particle burst
- **When:** 12.8–14.9.
- **What happens:** the balance digits **break into tiny black specks**, Telegram-spoiler style (12.8–13.4). The specks then become **3D spheres** that multiply into hundreds and fly at camera (13.8–14.9). The palette shifts to brand green, silver and a few gold. Large near-camera spheres are heavily bokeh'd. The UI behind is blurred about 20 px. It hands off to the next set when the particles clear.
- **How built:** glyph → point sampling (sample text alpha into about 400 points), jitter, then promote to instanced spheres with a z velocity toward camera. Add DOF by blurring each sprite by |z − focus|.
- **Why cool:** it is a transition, a reveal (hide → show) and a texture moment all at once. It also stays in palette, so it reads as brand rather than as a generic effect.

### 4.6 Hover magnify on avatar portholes
- **When:** 15.5–17.0.
- **What happens:** avatars sit in **glass portholes** (a thick clear ring with a highlight). The one under the bubble scales from 1.0 to about 1.18 over about 0.3 s with ease-out, gains a soft white glow ring, and **the 3D character inside waves**. The others stay put.
- **Why cool:** a person comes alive at the moment of selection. This is exactly our "Bub finds your match" beat.

### 4.7 Jelly 3D icon drop with coloured glow puddle
- **When:** 17.5–18.8.
- **What happens:** a big translucent green car (jelly/gummy material: SSS and a soft specular) drops onto its tile, squashes slightly, and spreads a **pink-lilac radial glow** under it, like light bleeding through a frosted surface.

### Recurring finish (applies to every move)
- **Depth of field:** always on. Foreground objects are blurred about 15–30 px at 720p. Focus pulls happen on transitions.
- **Motion blur:** only on fast moves (the whip, the curl, the particle fly-through). Slow moves are crisp.
- **Air dust:** tiny teal specks float in the air (visible at 12.4 s), which adds scale and atmosphere.
- **No grain or vignette to speak of.** The look is clean, high-key and soft.

## 5. Type

- **Almost no designed type.** All type is **UI type inside the objects** (Wallet Balance, $292.23, Bank transfers, Monthly Expenses), in a rounded geometric sans (Montserrat-like; italic on the wallet card). There are no supers, no headline cards and no logo lock-up.
- UI type is **embossed or printed on the slab**. It catches the same light as the surface and is defocused with it, so it never looks pasted on.
- Text "acting" = **numbers changing under the bubble** (counter roll about 0.4 s per value), **masked digits resolving**, and **digits dissolving to dust**. Type is treated as a material and a state, not as a caption.
- Holds: a UI label stays readable for 1.5–3 s (for example, "Bank transfers" 15.3–17.2).
- Lesson for us: our big headline cards ("How to make more friends", "New friends who share your values") are fine for a hook. But **inside the product beats the type should live on the 3D UI**, catch light, and change under Bub, rather than float beside the phone in flat 2D.

## 6. Colour, light, material

- **Palette:** brand greens (#1DB954-ish to #0E8F4A plus a lime highlight #C6F24A), seafoam/teal void (#9FD3CF → #CDEDEA), frosted white, and a lilac→pink secondary (#C9B6E8 / #F2C6DA) on the lower sheets and the second set. Gold (#E2A93B) is used sparingly as an accent ball.
- **Key light:** one large soft key from upper left, read as a **window-light gobo** (blurred bright rectangles on the backdrop at 1.4–3.5). A cool fill, very soft shadows, high key overall.
- **Materials:** matte frosted plastic slabs with rounded bevels and a thin bright rim; glossy jelly (SSS) icons; a crystal-clear thin-film glass bubble; glossy pearl spheres; photoreal wood on the desk.
- **Reflections:** the bubble is the only truly reflective and refractive object, so it owns the "wow". Everything else is soft-satin, which makes the bubble pop.

## 7. Tempo & sound

- About 8 shots in 23.5 s. Each shot is **one continuous eased camera move**. Cuts are disguised as whips, defocus, wipes or curls; only 21.7 is a flat hard cut.
- Music is bright electronic with transient hits recurring roughly on a 0.5 s grid (RMS peaks at 0.25, 2.25, 3.5, 4.0, 5.5, 7.25, 8.5, 9.75, 10.5, 11.0, 13.0, 17.75, 18.75, 22.5 s). UI SFX (ticks, swooshes) sit on top.
- **Two silence dips act as springboards:** 7.75–8.0 s (−40/−49 dB) right before the card cut at 8.5, and 16.5 s (−42 dB) before the curl at 17.4. The silence lands about 0.5–0.75 s before the visual hit.
- Energy curve: punch (desk burst), sustained (wallet), peak (eye toggle → particle burst, 12–15 s), breathe (bank transfers), punch (expenses), resolve (desk).

## 8. Steal for almost friends

1. **Bub = the glass bubble agent (values pick, Bub search, match).** In the current release, values are picked by a grey tap-dot on flat 2D chips (sheet_01, 0:09–0:10.5), and Bub is a separate character outside the phone. Change: Bub **rolls across the 3D phone UI** and is the thing that picks. Each value chip **magnifies and refracts inside Bub's body with RGB dispersion**, then pops up to the selected state when Bub passes. Implement as the UI-texture-through-bubble refraction lens (4.2).
2. **Exploded UI lift-off at phone entry (0:07–0:09).** Currently the phone just slides in, and its UI is a flat screen texture (sheet_01, 0:07–0:08.5). Change: the phone lands, then its chat bubbles and chips **lift 1–4 cm off the glass**, staggered by 2 frames with `easeOutBack`, with contact shadows on the screen. Do the reverse (layers settle back in) before the end mark.
3. **Hover magnify portholes for the match (0:19–0:22).** Currently the match is a yellow ring around a bubble in a busy field (sheet_02, 0:20–0:21.5). Change: as Bub passes candidate bubbles, each one under Bub **swells to 1.18× with a soft glow ring**, and the match keeps swelling. Inside, a **blurred hint of a person** or the value icon animates, the way the 3D avatars waved.
4. **Counter or state change under Bub for the unlock (0:41–0:50).** The current unlock is a padlock tap inside a flat card (sheet_04, 0:48.5–0:49.5). Change: Bub **rolls over the locked card**. Through Bub's lens you see the padlock refracted and split RGB, and on the far side the padlock is open. That one-pass "lens leaves a changed world behind" is the eye-toggle move (12.2 s) applied to the brand's best moment.
5. **Spoiler-dust → particle burst for "No names. No photos." and the reveal.** Anonymous chat could **spoiler-dust** the name and photo fields (black or brand-blue specks over the text). At the reveal the specks **burst into 3D bubbles flying at camera**, and when they clear, the faces are there. This replaces the current flat confetti at 0:51 (sheet_04).
6. **Curled-sheet chat stack for the 3 days (0:34–0:40).** Currently Day 1/2/3 is a phone that stays the same size with 2D type beside it (sheet_03). Change: three chat screens as **curled paper sheets** stacked back to front (Day 1 blue, Day 2 coral, Day 3 white frosted), un-curling one at a time on music hits.
7. **Silence dip before the big hits.** Drop the music to near silence for about 0.5–0.75 s before **SAME!!** and before the unlock. In the current release the hits arrive on a constant bed.
8. **Air dust and DOF everywhere.** Add fine floating specks and always-on shallow DOF on the 3D phone shots. Currently the phone shots are fully sharp, flat-lit and empty (sheet_02, 0:27–0:29.5).

## 9. Don't copy

- **The photoreal desk set.** It is a nice bookend but off-brand for us, and a realistic desk drags us into stock-photo territory. If we bookend, bookend with our own bubble world.
- **The cluttered floating-icon cloud (1.4–3.5).** Dozens of icons at once reads as an "AE template". It also risks the busy field our client already disliked; our bubble crowd must stay sparse and readable.
- **Brand green palette and fintech props:** not ours. Keep blue #3D4BE8-ish and coral.
- **Dense black spoiler-dust over large areas:** at our vertical scale, big speck fields can turn trypophobic. Keep it to text-sized patches and convert to round bubbles fast.
- **The 16:9 composition:** everything is staged wide. For 1080×1920, stack vertically. The bubble rolls *down* the UI, and the camera tilts rather than pans.
