**What it is:** a comeback teaser that is really a **logo build**. A hand-drawn red "X" mark is refracted into a 3D crystal. The crystal becomes a faceted four-point star (the X in TXT), which passes through a series of glass, jewel and felt "settings". Two sibling stars join it for the "+ X +" triplet, which resolves to a clean white end card with the band name.

---

## 2. Why it reads high-end

It is a single hero object made of **one convincing material**: a faceted crystal with real dispersion (rainbow on every facet edge), total internal reflection, and caustic light. The film keeps re-lighting and re-framing that object rather than adding more things. Each shot puts the star into a new **environment that exists only to show off the material**: god-rays in space, a black void with a halo, glass frame settings, a brilliant-cut lattice, blue felt (soft vs sharp), a light-speed burst, and a lavender sky. The rhythm is cut-dense, but each cut is hidden in a flash, a crystal wipe or a light streak, so the film reads as one continuous build to the logo. The end is a **light, white, calm card**: dark→light, chaos→order, which is exactly the "night→day" our client prefers.

## 4. Signature moves (特效)

### 4.1 Drawing → crystal refraction (the "mark becomes material" move)
- **When:** 1.9–2.9 s.
- **Frame by frame:** at 1.9 a clear crystal's edge enters from the upper left. Behind its facets the red X is **displaced and duplicated** per facet, each copy shifted a few percent. Facet edges split into **R / Y / M bands** (dispersion), and the red mark goes magenta and orange in the refracted copies. By 2.3 the crystal fills the frame and the flat red is gone, replaced by pink, yellow and white light play. At 2.7–2.9 the camera exits the back of the crystal into black space.
- **How built:** a 2D plate (the drawing) behind a CG crystal with refraction, plus a dispersion pass (three IORs, or a spectral render). In WebGL: render the "mark" layer to a texture. Draw a faceted SDF (or a low-poly mesh) and, per pixel, use the facet normal to offset the UV, sampling R/G/B with three different offsets (`k*0.96, k, k*1.04`). Facet-edge masks (where the SDF gradient flips) get a brightened rainbow band.
- **Why cool:** it is a visual sentence: rough human mark → precious refined object. The logo is **born in the material**.

### 4.2 Volumetric god-ray hero shot
- **When:** 3.0–7.2.
- **What happens:** a single wide diagonal beam (about 35° from vertical, upper right → lower left) with soft falloff. The hero crystal sits *in* the beam: its rim and edges burn to white (bloom), and the facets take a cyan core. Off-beam shards are lit only on their edges. **Floating dust and micro-shards** catch light inside the beam only. The parallax of near shards against the far ones gives scale.
- **How built:** an additive gradient quad for the beam, a noise-modulated dust sprite field masked by the beam, and a bloom threshold on edges. A cheap, very effective look.

### 4.3 Halo-ring spin reveal on black
- **When:** 7.3–8.4.
- **What happens:** the finished star sits dead centre with a **thin soft halo ring** (lilac, about 2× the star's diameter, 0.5 s fade) and four **long thin flare spikes** (a star filter). It **spins on its vertical axis**, so at the edge-on frames (7.6, 8.1) it collapses to a thin sliver of light and then expands again. That flicker of thin→wide reads as a shimmer.
- **Why cool:** a quiet beat before the build. Showing the object edge-on proves it has depth.

### 4.4 Jewel-setting carousel (match-cut on centre)
- **When:** 9.5–12.2.
- **What happens:** the star stays fixed at frame centre while **glass bezels change around it** every 0.3–0.6 s: a concave four-point frame, interlocking rings, a square bezel with diamond rivets that sparkle (four-point glints that flash for about 3 frames), an octagon, then a round brilliant. The backgrounds are soft periwinkle-to-cyan refracted light.
- **How built:** pre-built ring and frame meshes, swapped on beats, each entering with a scale 1.15 → 1.0 and a rotation of about 15°. In raymarching: SDF frames (rounded box minus box, polar-repeated petals) with glass shading. Rivet glints are a cross-shaped sprite with a fast attack and slow decay.
- **Why cool:** variety without losing the subject. Every 0.5 s the eye gets a new jewel, yet the logo never moves.

### 4.5 Soft vs sharp: crystal in felt with caustics
- **When:** 12.6–13.9.
- **What happens:** the hard, sparkling star sits in a cross-shaped gap between **thick, fuzzy navy felt blocks**. Blue light floods the gap, and **rainbow caustic arcs** (thin spectral bands) sweep across the felt as the camera rotates. A large lens-flare ring circles the star.
- **Why cool:** material contrast. The fuzzy matte surface makes the crystal look harder and brighter. This is the most "luxury product shot" moment.

### 4.6 Light-speed burst
- **When:** 15.2–16.2.
- **What happens:** a radial streak field (a zoom-blurred noise texture) with a white-hot core. The streaks are cyan, ultramarine and violet with **rainbow edge fringes**. The star tumbles about 90° through it with motion blur on its tips. It ends in a **cut to pure white** (16.3) and then a lavender sky (16.6), like stepping out of a tunnel into daylight.
- **How built:** polar coordinates. `streak = noise(angle * 80, radius * 2 - t * 6)`, multiplied by a radial falloff, then a chromatic offset per channel in radius. Classic, cheap and very energetic.

### 4.7 Laser streak pass-through
- **When:** 17.6–18.0 (about 12 frames).
- **What happens:** a long **pink-white neon streak** with speed-line fragments passes diagonally behind the star and *through* it. The star's facets **pick up the pink** for those frames (light interaction). A comet arc follows at 18.2.

### 4.8 Triplet ignition and halo
- **When:** 18.4–19.3.
- **What happens:** the side stars appear as **white bloom flares first** (18.4, overexposed), then resolve into the crystal (18.6). Around the trio: radial god-rays, a large **soft rainbow ring** (an ellipse; thin-film colours), and **floating prism-flare shards** (small rainbow smears scattered in a ring). Then everything **blooms to white**, and the end card appears on that white.

### Recurring finish
- **Dispersion on every edge.** No facet edge is ever plain white; there is always a thin spectral split.
- **Bloom:** heavy on highlights, with a white-hot core and a coloured spill.
- **Star-filter glints:** four-point sparkles on tips and rivets, 2–4 frames each.
- **Fine dust and micro-shards** in volumetric light.
- **DOF:** moderate; foreground shards are blurred.
- **No visible grain.**

## 5. Type

- **Type appears only at the end** (19.4 s onward) and on a pure white card. It is never laid over the CG.
- Order: crystal marks first (already there at the cut), then the **wordmark fades in grey→black** (19.5–20.0), then the subtitle (≈20.0), then the date line (≈20.0–20.5). The stagger is about 0.25 s per line, and each line fades over about 0.3 s with no motion.
- Hierarchy: wordmark about 2.6× the subtitle cap height, date smaller still. Everything is centred under the marks, in a condensed geometric sans with **custom "+" glyphs replacing the T's**, so the logo's "+" stars carry into the letters.
- Hold: the full card holds about **3.5 s** after the last line lands.
- "Type born in the material": here it is the **logo glyph (X) itself** that is born. A hand-drawn mark turns into crystal, and the type is a quiet caption afterwards. The lesson: let the **mark** do the 特效, and keep the words calm and legible on light.

## 6. Colour, light, material

- **Palette:** periwinkle/ultramarine (#4B5BD6 → #7A86E8), lavender (#B9AEE8), cyan (#4FD6E8), white-hot cores, and accents of pink (#F07AB8) and red-orange (the opening mark, #E5432A). The dark sections are indigo and black-green; the final third is all light (lavender sky → white).
- **Key light:** hard, directional and volumetric (god-ray, gobo channels, laser streaks). The crystal is always backlit or edge-lit, so facets glow.
- **Material:** clear crystal/diamond with **dispersion, internal reflection and caustics**; thin-film rainbow rings; contrasting matte fuzzy felt; soft cloud skies.
- **What reflects:** only the crystal. Everything around it is either a light source (beams, streaks, sky) or a matte foil (felt), which keeps the hero readable.

## 7. Tempo & sound

- **Structure:** 7.3 s slow intro (one evolving shot), a 1 s breath (black star spin), then about 10 s of rapid cuts every 0.3–1.2 s, then a 1 s resolve, then a 4.5 s end card.
- **Sound envelope (RMS, 0.25 s windows):** near silence to 1.9 s (−40 to −48 dB); **hit at 2.0** when the drawing turns to crystal (−22 dB); a steady −21 to −25 dB to 7.25; a **dip −27 to −32 dB at 7.5–9.0** (the black star beat); a climb to −15/−16 dB from 9.5 to 17.0 (the settings, felt and light-speed sections); decay from 17.5; −32 dB at 19.0; silence by 22.5. The end card plays over a dying reverb, with no button sting.
- **Lesson:** a big swell that *decays into* the logo. The quiet end card feels expensive because the sound lets go.

1. **End mark born in the material (0:62–0:66).** In the current release the blue+coral pair just appears as flat glossy spheres on a pale gradient, with a pop-in wordmark (sheet_05, 1:02–1:03). Change: the two bubbles **slide over the faces or crowd and refract them into rainbow-split copies** (4.1). They resolve into the shared-wall end mark with **dispersion on the shared wall** (a thin R/G/B split along the meniscus), a **soft rainbow thin-film ring** around both (4.8), and a 3-frame four-point glint on each highlight. Then bloom to white and fade the type in line by line (wordmark, then the tagline, about 0.25 s apart, no motion).
2. **The hook mark → material (0:00–0:03).** Open on a **hand-drawn "almost / friends" scribble** or a rough circle on off-white paper, then a glass bubble rolls over it and refracts it into rainbow copies, so the drawing becomes the liquid bubble world. The current hook starts with Bub's eyes on a pale field (sheet_01, 0:00). This gives frame 0 a human mark plus a material transformation within 2 s.
3. **God-ray hero for Bub's search (0:15–0:22).** The current search shot is a dense, evenly lit pastel bubble crowd (sheet_02). Change: **one diagonal volumetric beam** that Bub swims through; only bubbles inside the beam get bright edges, dust and sparkle, and the rest go soft and dim. It keeps the crowd but gives the frame a subject and depth. Stay light: a warm cream beam on a pale lavender-blue ground, not indigo space.
4. **Match = halo-ring spin (≈0:22).** When Bub finds the match, freeze the crowd and **centre the match bubble with a thin lilac halo ring and four flare spikes**, spinning slowly. Add a 1 s audio breath (dip about 8–10 dB) before SAME!!. This replaces the current yellow outline ring (sheet_02, 0:20–0:21.5).
5. **Unlock or reveal via light-speed burst (0:49–0:51).** The current unlock → "You're both in!" goes from a padlock to a white fade to confetti (sheet_04, 0:49.5–0:51). Change: the padlock opens and the camera flies **through a radial streak tunnel in brand blue and coral with rainbow fringes** (about 1 s), bleaches to white, and lands on the faces in daylight. That is our night→day in one move without a dark world.
6. **Laser streak "connection" (friends gather, 0:56–0:62).** A pink-coral neon streak passes through the paired bubbles and **tints their facets and highlights for about 12 frames**, then branches to each new friend bubble as it arrives. This replaces the current static scatter of face circles (sheet_04/05, 0:57–1:01.5).
7. **Bezel match-cut carousel for values (0:09–0:12).** Keep the chosen value bubble fixed at centre and **swap glass "settings" around it on beats every 0.4 s** (Family → Career → Adventure icons as glass rings or frames, each scaling 1.15 → 1.0 with a rivet glint). It is varied, but legible.
8. **Dispersion and glints as global finish.** Add thin spectral edge splits on every bubble rim and Bub's eyes' highlights, plus 2–4 frame four-point glints on the hits. This is cheap in our shader and is the single most "特效" upgrade vs the current clean-but-plain bubbles.

## 9. Don't copy

- **Indigo or black space and the black void (3.0–8.4).** A dark world was rejected twice. Borrow the *beam and halo* lighting ideas but stage them on light grounds, or use dark only as a sub-second dip that resolves to white.
- **Sharp shard and star geometry.** Our language is round liquid bubbles; a four-point star or faceted gems would be off-brand. Translate "facets" into **thin-film bands and caustics** on spheres.
- **Navy felt:** the contrast idea is good, but fuzzy dark textile is moody. If we want soft vs sharp, use a pale linen or cotton surface.
- **Cut density of 0.3–0.6 s for 10 s straight:** fine for a teaser, but it would bury our story beats and UI. Use rapid carousels only for 1–2 s inserts.
- **Type only at the end:** our film needs type throughout (hook, steps). Keep that, but take the *calm on white, line-by-line* end-card behaviour.
- **The red crayon specifically:** borrow the "rough mark" idea in our blue and coral.
