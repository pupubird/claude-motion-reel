## 2. Why it reads high-end

It reads high-end because of **contrast in dynamic range and in mood**. Saturated, bloomed, light-beam hero scenes (deep purple base, crossed white-yellow light shafts, glowing 3D props) alternate with sterile white product scenes, and every change between the two lands on a musical hit or a short silence. Nothing ever sits still. Each shot has a camera move, and the fast moves carry real **directional motion blur** (subframe-accumulated, not a CSS blur). That one detail is what makes flat UI read as "filmed". Type is never just faded in. It **decodes** (random glyphs that resolve, letter-spacing that collapses, a colour gradient that sweeps through), so even a three-word line feels like an effect. Light is treated as a material: bloom halos around every bright edge, a heavy vignette, and coloured rim light on the card edge.

## 4. Signature moves (特效)

### 4.1 Decode-scramble type with a gradient sweep (0.0–1.2, 16.6 "0.00098 BTC", 19.5–21.3, 31.4–33.8)
- **Frame by frame:** characters reveal left to right with about a **1-frame stagger** (≈30 chars/s). Before a character locks it shows random lowercase glyphs that change every frame (0.8 s still: "spend bozui j o…"). Unlocked glyphs are spaced at roughly **+0.3–0.5 em tracking**, and that tracking collapses to 0 as each glyph locks, so the word visibly tightens. Locked glyphs carry a horizontal gradient (orange → hot pink → magenta), and the unlocked tail sits at lower opacity with ~4–8 px blur. One line takes **about 0.5 s (15 frames)** to resolve and holds 0.3–0.6 s before the next. In "No switching" the leading word is plain white and only the key word gets the gradient and scramble.
- **Build (Remotion/Canvas):** for character *i* at frame *f*: `lock = f >= start + i*1`. If not locked, `glyph = charset[floor(random(`${i}-${floor(f/1)}`)*n)]` (Remotion `random()` is deterministic per seed). `letterSpacing = interpolate(f - (start+i), [-6,0], [0.4,0], clamp)`, `filter: blur(interpolate(...,[ -6,0],[6,0]))`, `opacity` 0.3→1. Apply the gradient with `background-clip:text` across the whole word so locked letters reveal a fixed gradient. In our WebGL pipeline it is the same logic per glyph quad in an SDF/MSDF text shader, with blur done as SDF softness.
- **Why it's cool:** it turns three words into an event. The tracking collapse reads as "something snapping into place", a stronger idea than a fade.

### 4.2 Crossed light shafts plus bloom burst behind the hero object (1.5–3.3, 27.5–31.0)
- **Frame by frame:** on the 1.55 s hit, two wide soft-edged beams form an **X behind the card**, white-yellow at the core with an orange/magenta falloff, plus a third magenta shaft lower right. The violet base brightens to orange-pink in the top half. Every bright object gets a **wide bloom halo** (≈30–60 px), and there is a strong vignette (corners ~70% darker). Beams rotate slowly (a few °/s) while the camera pushes.
- **Build:** 2–3 full-screen quads with a 1D gradient across their width (`smoothstep` falloff on both edges), additive blend, rotated ±35°. Then a separable Gaussian bloom pass (threshold ~0.7, 3 mip levels) and a radial vignette. In Remotion this is likely `<AbsoluteFill>` divs with `linear-gradient` and `mix-blend-mode: screen` plus a `filter: blur(40px)` duplicate. In WebGL it is a proper bloom on the HDR buffer.
- **Why it's cool:** it is a **light event**. The scene gets brighter on the beat, which is the fastest way to make a product moment feel like a reveal.

### 4.3 Subframe motion blur on UI whip pans (9.0–15.6, 19.5–21.3, 25.6)
- **Frame by frame:** a whip covers a camera travel of 400–900 px in **6–8 frames** with ease-in-out (in the 10.6 still, text is smeared vertically ~15 px; in 12.6 everything is smeared horizontally ~60 px while the cursor ring stays crisper). At rest the UI is pin-sharp. Scale on close-ups is ~2.5–3× actual UI size, so type is huge on screen.
- **Build:** Remotion ships `<CameraMotionBlur shutterAngle={180} samples={10}>`, which renders N subframes and averages them. That is exactly what this looks like (ghosted copies, not a Gaussian). In our pipeline: render 8–16 subframes per output frame at 60 fps (shutter 180° → spread subframes across half a frame interval) and accumulate in a float buffer. We already render in headless Chrome frame by frame, so this is a render-loop change, not an art change.
- **Why it's cool:** blur is the cue that says "camera" rather than "slideshow". It is the cheapest single upgrade for every phone and UI shot in our film.

### 4.4 Hollow gradient ring cursor with click squash (9.5–15.0)
- A ~40 px hollow ring with a conic gradient (pink→orange→yellow), a soft outer glow and a white fill. On click it scales 1→0.8→1.05→1 over ~8 frames and emits a faint expanding disc (12.6 still: a large peach disc ~600 px wide at ~10% opacity behind the card). The pressed element tints peach.
- **Build:** a DOM/Canvas element, `spring({damping: 12})` for the squash, and a ripple disc whose `scale` goes 0→1 and `opacity` 0.15→0 over 18 frames.

### 4.5 "Hot while moving, neutral at rest" number counter (12.5–13.5, 25.9–27.0)
- The amount steps $33 → $53 → $73 → $93 → $100. Steps are discrete (not smooth), about 4 frames apart, and each is **coloured in a pink-red gradient while changing**, then turns plain black when it lands. Digits blur slightly between steps.
- **Build:** `Math.round(interpolate(f,[a,b],[33,100], easing.out))` quantised to steps of 20, with colour = gradient while `f<b`, else ink.
- **Why it's cool:** colour signals that the value is live. It is a nice semantic use of the accent colour.

### 4.6 Object-carry transition: icon between words becomes the next scene's avatar (24.0–26.3)
- "Same account" sits centred on white. The words push apart (≈60 px each) and a 140 px gradient bank-icon disc scales 0→1.1→1 between them with a little sparkle. In the quiet gap the icon holds. On the 25.25 hit the icon **lifts and flies up-right**, the words smear sideways and vanish, and the icon lands as the pink avatar in the "Send to" row of the next UI, which builds around it.
- **Build:** one persistent element across two "scenes". Interpolate its position/scale from layout A to layout B with spring easing and motion blur on. The next scene's UI fades and slides in around its final position. This is a classic Remotion trick: keep the element in a parent `<AbsoluteFill>` above both `<Sequence>`s.
- **Why it's cool:** the viewer's eye is handed from one shot to the next. It makes the cut invisible and the product feel continuous.

### 4.7 Light ribbon wrapping the phone (21.9–24.0)
- A thick (~70 px at 1080p) tube-like stroke with heavy blur on its edges, coloured orange at one end and coral-pink at the other. It sweeps around the phone in an S/helix and passes **in front at the top and behind at the bottom**, which sells depth. It draws on over ~0.5 s and keeps undulating. Tiles below get green "Paid" pills with three green 4-point sparkles.
- **Build:** a 3D spline tube (Three.js `TubeGeometry` with an emissive gradient) that intersects the phone in depth, or a 2D path split into front and back halves around the phone layer. Use `stroke-dashoffset` draw-on and an 8–12 px blur.
- **Why it's cool:** it is a single graphic gesture that adds colour and depth to a white scene with no clutter.

### 4.8 Stadium ring tunnel (3.3–4.9)
- 8–10 concentric rounded rectangles drawn as 2 px grey lines on black, seen in perspective so they read as a tunnel or track. The card spins through with a magenta underglow, and props pass close to the lens, out of focus.
- **Build:** line loops on z-stepped planes and a perspective camera craning up. This is a cheap, graphic way to make a black void feel like a space.

### 4.9 Glass notification over a 3D globe (16.4–19.4)
- A textured Earth spins and decelerates. A rounded notification pill with **backdrop blur ~20 px** and 70% white slides in over it. Flags pop as tiny pins with a ring pulse. The notification's text is still decoding as it moves.
- **Build:** Three.js sphere with a day texture plus a cloud layer. The glass is a framebuffer blur sampled under a rounded rect (we already do refraction in the raymarcher, so a blur-sample is easy).

## 5. Type

- **Family:** a neutral grotesk (SF Pro / Inter-like), mostly **Regular to Medium**, never heavy. "anywhere." is the one bold usage. Lines are centred in a 16:9 frame at about **5–7% of frame height** for statement lines ("No waiting" is ~9%).
- **Placement:** always centre-frame. The hero object occludes the text: the card slices "anywhere." and sits on top of it, so the text lives *in* the 3D space (behind props) rather than on a UI layer.
- **Entry:** decode-scramble (4.1) or blur-to-sharp scale-in ("anywhere." arrives ~150% size with ~40 px blur and resolves in ~5 frames). UI words appear word by word ("Payment ⟶ successful", "Processing ⟶ transaction") with a 2–3 frame stagger.
- **Exit:** motion-blurred whip off, or swallowed by the hero object passing in front.
- **Hold:** short. A statement line is readable for **0.4–0.8 s** after resolve ("spend anything." ≈0.5 s, "No waiting" ≈0.6 s, endcard tagline ≈1.5 s).
- **Colour:** white for neutral words. The key word gets the orange→pink→magenta gradient. On white scenes the ink is 80% black.

## 6. Colour, light, material

- **Hero world:** violet base (#2a0f6e→#140a3a), warm orange/peach highlight, crossed beams in cream-yellow, hot magenta accents, lime "$", cyan gem. Very saturated, with bloom on everything above ~0.7 luminance and a heavy vignette.
- **Product world:** pure white #fff with a soft grey vignette in 3D shots. UI is iOS-like, with blue/orange crypto icons.
- **Black interludes:** #000 / #1b1b1b with thin grey line work and white 4-point sparkles.
- **Materials:** the card is matte black with a glossy rim catching a magenta or gold edge light. Coins are glossy gold with a fat bevel. The props are toy-like 3D (clay-ish, specular hotspots). The globe is photoreal. The ribbon is emissive.
- **Key light:** a back or top-back key (the beams) makes rim light and bloom. Products are never front-lit flat in the hero world.

## 7. Tempo & sound

- The music is busy (hits every 0.3–0.45 s, ~140–160 BPM feel). Every visual action (a whip, a pill pop, a flag, a counter step) lands on a hit.
- **Silence as a transition tool.** The bed dips or drops out right before four section changes: 8.5 s (−25 dB), 12.0 s (−69 dB for ~0.3 s), 19.0 s (−26 dB) and 24.5–25.25 s (−40 to −48 dB for ~0.7 s). The next scene then lands on a hard hit. That "breath before the drop" is what makes the cuts feel intentional.
- **Energy curve:** high open (0–3 s), drop to studio calm (5–9 s), mid UI section (9–19 s), second high (19.5–21.3 "No switching / No waiting"), calm white (21.9–27 s), final high (27.5–31 s), quiet logo.
- It alternates high and low about every 4–6 s, never more than ~6 s in one register.

## 9. Don't copy

- **The black and violet worlds as a base.** "Dark ink worlds" is a known rejection, and our brand is pale sky to peach. Take the *light event* (beams, bloom, saturation spike) and play it over a brightened, saturated version of our own palette for a fraction of a second. Don't take the purple void.
- **The 3D prop confetti** (coins, $, gems, lightning) cluttering the frame. Our equivalents would be generic floating emoji. Bub and the bubbles are the props.
- **The thin, Regular-weight statement type.** At 1080×1920 on a phone, thin lines at ~6% of height won't read. Keep our bold rounded type and borrow only the *decode behaviour*.
- **Busy 16:9 compositions** with pills scattered on four sides of the card (7.4–9 s). In 9:16 that becomes noise and trends toward HUD clutter.
- **Text the hero object occludes** ("anywhere." half-hidden behind the card). It is cool in the reference, but our client asked for big, legible type, so occlusion should only ever be brief and in motion.
- **The photoreal globe.** It is a stock-feeling asset and adds nothing to a friendship story.
